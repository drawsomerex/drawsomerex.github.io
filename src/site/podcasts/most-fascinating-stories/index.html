Episode 10: The Pig War of 1859: The Standoff Over a Rooting Hog

In June 1859, on San Juan Island in the Pacific Northwest borderlands of the Oregon Territory, an American settler shot a British Berkshire pig rooting in his potato patch. The dispute escalated into a military standoff with five British warships, 167 guns, and Captain George Pickett's American troops, nearly sparking war between global superpowers.

In 532 AD, rival chariot racing factions in Constantinople united to overthrow Emperor Justinian, burning half the capital to the ground in the Nika Riots. As Justinian prepared to flee into exile, his wife Empress Theodora famously declared that royal purple made the finest burial shroud, turning the tide of imperial history.

If a foreign adversary launched an amphibious invasion of either the Atlantic or Pacific coast, the United States Army could not even transport troops and supplies across its own continent to defend the nation.
